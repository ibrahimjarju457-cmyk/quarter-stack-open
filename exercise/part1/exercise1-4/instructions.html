Running a Portable Generator Safely

This sheet is for anyone who has to run a petrol generator at home during a power cut. Read it before you start the engine, every time, even if you have done it before.

What you need

  • The generator, with its manual or label within reach
  • Fresh fuel in an approved fuel can
  • Engine oil, to top up if the level is low
  • An outdoor-rated extension cord, in good condition
  • A working CO alarm inside the house
  • A dry, flat, open spot outside, at least 6 m from doors, windows and vents

Steps

  1. Carry the generator outside to dry, flat ground at least 6 m from any door, window or vent, with the exhaust pointing away from the house. Never run it indoors, in a garage, or on a porch: the exhaust contains deadly CO.
  2. With the engine off and cool to the touch, check the oil and fuel levels and top up if needed.
  3. Switch off and unplug everything you plan to power.
  4. Start the generator as shown on its label and let it run for 2 min before you connect anything.
  5. Plug in one appliance at a time, using the outdoor-rated cord. Keep the total below the generator’s rated output, for example 3 kW. Never plug the generator into a wall socket: it can send power back into the grid and kill a utility worker.
  6. To refuel, unplug everything and switch the engine off. Add fuel only after the engine has cooled for at least 15 min. Fuel poured onto a hot engine can ignite.

If something goes wrong

If anyone has a headache, dizziness, nausea or confusion while the generator is running, switch it off, get everyone into fresh air at once, and call your local emergency number: these are signs of CO poisoning. If the generator catches fire, leave it, move everyone away and call the fire service; do not throw water on it.
